01 / DECODE THE QUESTION

What is the examiner asking?

Before writing, identify the statement you must assess and the bigger historical outcome every argument must explain.

HISTORY / LONG-ANSWER QUESTION

“To what extent were the different aims of Indian groups in 1857 the main reason why the War of Independence was short-lived? Explain your answer.”

14marks
Examiner statement Different aims of Indians

The proposition you must consider—not merely repeat.

Main theme Failure of the War of Independence

Every explanation must link to why the revolt was short-lived.

Build every developed paragraph
Reason→Effect→Failure

Do not stop at naming a reason. Explain its consequence for the revolt.

PAPER 01 / UNDERDEVELOPED

Knowledge, listed.

Mentions reasons. Rarely explains their effect.

4/14Illustrative

01The War of Independence in 1857 was short-lived because Indians had different aims. Hindus and Muslims had different interests. People were divided by caste and religion. Many Indian states did not take part. There were different groups of people across India and they did not all agree. The Nizam of Hyderabad and Sikh princes backed the British. Unity was very important in a war.×

Only identifying points. Why did these differences help the British defeat the revolt?

02There was also no common leader. Bahadur Shah Zafar, Nana Sahib and Rani of Jhansi were important leaders. They were famous people with their own interests. The Indians did not have a proper plan. A war needs planning and a leader, and they did not have one leader for everyone.

Names are not analysis. Show what a lack of command and planning actually caused.

03The Indians did not have enough money, food or weapons. They had some money from British treasuries but it was not enough. They also had communication problems. Soldiers need food and equipment to fight properly, and these things were needed in this war.

Another list. Connect weak supplies and coordination to the short duration of the war.

04The British had modern weapons and good soldiers. Their commanders were trained and disciplined. The army was regular and stronger. They were better at fighting, and so they won.

Some relevant knowledge, little development. Explain British military advantages.

05In conclusion, all these reasons caused the war to fail.

No judgement. The question asks whether different aims were the main cause—not simply for a list of causes.

PAPER 02 / DEVELOPED & EVALUATED

Knowledge, explained.

Develops causes, links consequences, reaches a judgement.

12/14Illustrative

01Different aims among Indian groups were a major reason the War of Independence was short-lived because they prevented a united response to British forces. Religious and caste divisions, as well as differing interests among Hindus and Muslims, reduced cooperation. Many states stayed out, while the Nizam of Hyderabad and Sikh princes supported the British. Consequently, rebel resistance remained scattered and British forces could defeat groups separately.✓

E1: Developed argument. Specific examples lead to a consequence, then a direct link to the question.

02However, the absence of common leadership was another major weakness. Bahadur Shah Zafar, Nana Sahib and the Rani of Jhansi had separate interests, and no shared political or military plan united the rebels. Their actions were not properly coordinated, whereas British forces acted with clearer strategy. Without united direction, rebel resistance lost momentum, making the revolt easier to suppress.✓

E2: Explanation and link. The answer shows how fragmented command affected the outcome.

03Limited resources further shortened the struggle. Money obtained from British treasuries was not enough to sustain the uprising; feeding fighters and supplying weapons remained difficult. Poor communication networks also prevented coordinated movements. These problems weakened the rebels' ability to continue fighting effectively.✓

E3: Sustained causation. Resource shortages are shown to limit the duration and effectiveness of resistance.

04British military strength made these weaknesses more damaging. The British had modern weapons, professionally trained troops and skilled, disciplined commanders. Their more organised response enabled them to exploit divided opposition. Thus, the rebels faced an opponent better prepared to defeat them.✓

E4: Balanced alternative. British superiority is weighed as a separate explanation for the quick defeat.

05Overall, different aims were a leading reason because they prevented the rebels from building a united movement. Yet disunity alone does not fully explain why the war was short-lived: poor leadership, inadequate supplies and British strength also mattered. Therefore, different aims were responsible to a large extent, but they were most damaging in combination with these other weaknesses.✓

Evaluative conclusion. Directly answers “to what extent?” and qualifies the judgement using other factors.

EXAMINER'S LENS

What makes the difference?

The weaker response names several correct ideas without showing how they caused the revolt to fail. The stronger response builds a chain of reasoning and finishes with a supported judgement.

Four explanations. One judgement.

04 / WHY THE MARKS DIFFER

The difference isn't length.
It's reasoning.
